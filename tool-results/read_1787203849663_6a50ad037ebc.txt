    69→    69→# 2. THE NORTH STAR
    70→    70→
    71→    71→The following principles are immutable unless explicitly changed through architecture governance.
    72→    72→
    73→    73→- Communication is transport-independent.
    74→    74→- Identity is independent of channel.
    75→    75→- Intent is independent of transport.
    76→    76→- Communication Bundles are the fundamental routable object.
    77→    77→- Matrix is the global/federated communication fabric.
    78→    78→- DTN is the offline/edge communication fabric.
    79→    79→- External networks are adapters.
    80→    80→- Gateways connect edge networks to external networks.
    81→    81→- Routing operates over capabilities, policy and resources.
    82→    82→- Applications consume the protocol; they do not define it.
    83→    83→- No single transport is mandatory.
    84→    84→- Internet availability is an optimization, not a prerequisite.
    85→    85→- Offline operation is a first-class architectural requirement.
    86→    86→- Security and privacy are protocol properties, not UI features.
    87→    87→- Architecture changes require explicit approval.

   143→   143→# 4. THREE FUNDAMENTAL PROTOCOL PRIMITIVES
   144→   144→
   145→   145→## 4.1 Universal Identity
   146→   146→
   147→   147→Identity represents the communication entity independently of transport.
   148→   148→
   149→   149→A person is not:
   150→   150→
   151→   151→```text
   152→   152→WhatsApp number
   153→   153→Email address
   154→   154→Phone number
   155→   155→Matrix ID
   156→   156→```
   157→   157→
   158→   158→Those are channel identities associated with a universal identity.
   159→   159→
   160→   160→Conceptually:
   161→   161→
   162→   162→```text
   163→   163→UniversalIdentity
   164→   164→    │
   165→   165→    ├── MatrixIdentity
   166→   166→    ├── WhatsAppIdentity
   167→   167→    ├── SMSIdentity
   168→   168→    ├── EmailIdentity
   169→   169→    ├── TelegramIdentity
   170→   170→    ├── SocialIdentity
   171→   171→    └── DeviceIdentity
   172→   172→```
   173→   173→
   174→   174→Identity resolution must be explicit, authenticated, privacy-aware, and policy-controlled.
   175→   175→
   176→   176→Never make a channel identifier the universal identity primitive.
   177→   177→

   180→   180→# 5. COMMUNICATION INTENT
   181→   181→
   182→   182→The sender expresses what they want to accomplish.
   183→   183→
   184→   184→Examples:
   185→   185→
   186→   186→- SEND_MESSAGE
   187→   187→- NOTIFY
   188→   188→- REQUEST_RESPONSE
   189→   189→- DELIVER_DOCUMENT
   190→   190→- SEND_MEDIA
   191→   191→- EMERGENCY_ALERT
   192→   192→- SYNC_CONVERSATION
   193→   193→
   194→   194→Intent may contain:
   195→   195→
   196→   196→- priority
   197→   197→- TTL
   198→   198→- maximum cost
   199→   199→- latency requirement
   200→   200→- privacy requirement
   201→   201→- delivery requirement
   202→   202→- payload constraints
   203→   203→- preferred transports
   204→   204→- fallback policy
   205→   205→
   206→   206→The application says:
   207→   207→
   208→   208→> "Deliver this."
   209→   209→
   210→   210→It does not say:
   211→   211→
   212→   212→> "Call WhatsApp API."
   213→   213→
   214→   214→Transport selection belongs to the protocol/routing layer.

   562→   562→# 14. OFFLINE COMMUNICATION
   563→   563→
   564→   564→Offline communication is not an emergency feature.
   565→   565→
   566→   566→It is part of the core protocol.
   567→   567→
   568→   568→The system must support:
   569→   569→
   570→   570→- ONLINE
   571→   571→- DEGRADED
   572→   572→- OFFLINE
   573→   573→- PARTITIONED
   574→   574→- EMERGENCY
   575→   575→
   576→   576→### ONLINE
   577→   577→
   578→   578→Prefer appropriate Internet paths.
   579→   579→
   580→   580→### DEGRADED
   581→   581→
   582→   582→Reduce payload requirements and prefer efficient/reliable transports.
   583→   583→
   584→   584→### OFFLINE
   585→   585→
   586→   586→Use local transports and store-and-forward.
   587→   587→
   588→   588→### PARTITIONED
   589→   589→
   590→   590→Allow messages to remain queued until connectivity opportunities appear.
   591→   591→
   592→   592→### EMERGENCY
   593→   593→
   594→   594→Prioritize small, urgent communication and suppress nonessential traffic.
   595→   595→
   596→   596→---

   599→   599→
   600→   600→Never use simplistic:
   601→   601→
   602→   602→```text

   604→   604→```

   606→   606→Delivery must be modeled explicitly.

   609→   609→

   615→   615→GATEWAY_REACHED

   619→   619→```

   621→   621→Failure states may include:
   622→   622→
   623→   623→- EXPIRED

   625→   625→- POLICY_BLOCKED
   626→   626→- NO_ROUTE
   627→   627→- CHANNEL_UNAVAILABLE
   628→   628→- GATEWAY_UNAVAILABLE
   629→   629→- DESTINATION_UNKNOWN
   630→   630→
   631→   631→Offline delivery requires distinguishing:
   632→   632→
   633→   633→> "The message left my device"
   634→   634→
   635→   635→from:
   636→   636→
   637→   637→> "The message reached the recipient."

   641→   641→# 16. ENCRYPTION AND TRUST
   642→   642→
   643→   643→Relays should not inherently require plaintext access.
   644→   644→
   645→   645→The security model must distinguish:
   646→   646→
   647→   647→- sender
   648→   648→- recipient
   649→   649→- relay
   650→   650→- gateway
   651→   651→- external channel
   652→   652→
   653→   653→A relay should generally be able to forward an opaque encrypted bundle without learning its contents.
   654→   654→
   655→   655→Do not equate:
   656→   656→
   657→   657→```text
   658→   658→transport authentication
   659→   659→```
   660→   660→
   661→   661→with:
   662→   662→
   663→   663→```text
   664→   664→end-to-end confidentiality
   665→   665→```
   666→   666→
   667→   667→Threat-model:
   668→   668→
   669→   669→- malicious relays
   670→   670→- malicious gateways
   671→   671→- replay
   672→   672→- bundle duplication
   673→   673→- Sybil nodes
   674→   674→- spam
   675→   675→- flooding
   676→   676→- resource exhaustion
   677→   677→- identity impersonation
   678→   678→- routing manipulation
   679→   679→- malicious channel adapters
   680→   680→- metadata leakage
   681→   681→- compromised devices
   682→   682→- stale routing information
   683→   683→
   684→   684→Never invent cryptography.
   685→   685→
   686→   686→Use established primitives and libraries.
   687→   687→
   688→   688→---
   689→   689→
   690→   690→# 17. RESOURCE-CONSTRAINED COMMUNICATION

   692→   692→The protocol must understand constrained environments.
   693→   693→
   694→   694→A communication intent may specify:
   695→   695→
   696→   696→- MAX_BYTES
   697→   697→- MAX_COST
